Where the internet actually runs.

About 99% of intercontinental data travels through roughly 600 submarine cables lying on the seabed. This atlas tracks a curated, fully-sourced subset of 25 major systems — their routes, their owners, every documented cut since 2006, and the narrow geographic corridors where a disproportionate share of that traffic converges.

Routes are drawn between each system's documented landing points as gently-bowed lines, not a projection of the real seabed path — landing points are a representative subset per system, not exhaustive. Land outline: Natural Earth 110m via world-atlas (public domain).

Chokepoints

Narrow corridors where cable systems physically converge, so a small-area incident has an outsized blast radius. Ranges are cited from public analysis, not a single authoritative count — different trade sources count cables differently, so a range is shown honestly instead of a falsely precise figure.
